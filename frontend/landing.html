Example response

Begin with one practice you can return to.

Guidance, not psak

Set apart a small piece of time. Choose a dependable point on Friday evening when work and ordinary tasks stop. The Torah frames Shabbat as both remembering creation and practicing freedom from servitude.

Add delight, not pressure. A meal, candles, a song, a walk, or unrushed time with someone you love can make the distinction tangible. Isaiah links sacred time with delight, and rabbinic tradition praises honoring Shabbat.

Let consistency come before completeness. Start with what you can sustain. Learn what feels meaningful, then ask which practice you want to deepen next.

A reference is useful when you know what it means.

rebbe.dev can show two kinds of source references. The distinction matters.

Locally matched source
The system found relevant material in its local text library and matched the reference to that retrieved context. This supports traceability, but it is not a guarantee that every claim has been verified.
Model-knowledge citation
The reference comes from the model's learned knowledge rather than a local text match. Treat it as a lead for further study and verify it before relying on details.

A few useful answers.

Is rebbe.dev a rabbi?

No. It is a guidance companion that helps you explore sources and perspectives. It cannot issue binding psak or replace a rabbi who knows you.

Which Jewish perspective does it use?

The default is progressive Modern Orthodox, and signed-in users can share a denominational context. A response may still present multiple positions when the sources meaningfully differ.

Are all citations verified?

No. The app distinguishes a source matched in the local library from a citation based on model knowledge. A local match improves traceability but does not establish claim-level verification.

Does the service close for Shabbat?

No. The app may show a calendar notice for Shabbat or yom tov based on approximate local timing, but it remains available.

Who can see my conversations?

Ordinary users can access only their own signed-in history. Authorized administrators may review content for operations, moderation, and safety.
